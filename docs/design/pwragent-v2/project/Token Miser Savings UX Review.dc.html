UX review · 2026-09-02 · Token Miser Savings lens

The Savings lens lost its list

The lens answers one question: what did gating buy, and where. The headline still answers it. Everything under the headline has grown into six fixed-height bands of monospace prose, and at the window size it actually runs in they add up to more than the window, so the one scrollable, explorable part of the screen is squeezed to a single row.

Where the chart went. The chart you remember on the top right is the Incidents lens' When it went turn strip. That lens lays its summary out as a two-column grid, headline left and timeline right. The Savings lens never had a chart until #1926 added Context by turn yesterday, and it went in as a full-width band between the hero and the results, with its own top and bottom rules and a 72 px plot. The fix is to give the Savings lens the same two-column hero the Incidents lens already has.

Findings

What is wrong, in order of damage

Blocking
F1The results list gets whatever height is left, and nothing is left

Every band above the list is flex: none or has a fixed plot height. The list is the only flex: 1 child with min-height: 0, so it absorbs the whole shortfall. At 700 px tall the fixed bands and their gaps use roughly 600 px before the results section starts, and the list, which is the only part of this screen you can actually explore, renders one row with a scrollbar. It reads as broken rather than as a scrolling list.

Height budget at 1000 × 700, from the 2× screenshot: chrome 70 · hero ≈160 (the three terms wrap under the confidence chip because their 560 px minimum plus the left column does not fit) · two caption lines ≈40 · Context by turn ≈105 · Context boundaries ≈75 · Code Mode ≈55 · gaps and padding ≈95 → ≈600 px spent before the results header, leaving a header and one 48 px row.

Fix: give the list a floor (three rows, about 160 px) and make the bands above it the thing that yields. Move the chart into the hero's right column, collapse the three prose sections into one-line rows, and let the operator drag the boundary.

F2Nothing above the list collapses, and there is no grip

Context boundaries and Code Mode behavior are reference data. They matter on the day you are checking a compaction or debugging a dispatch cluster, and not otherwise. They are always fully expanded, and the divider above the results is a plain rule, so the operator has no recourse: they cannot fold what they are not reading and cannot pull the list taller.

Fix: each secondary section becomes a details row whose closed state carries its two or three headline numbers. A row-resize grip sits between the details stack and the results. Open state and grip height persist per machine in the renderer, never federated, the same rule as drafts.

Should fix
F3Eight lines of dot-joined monospace with no hierarchy

Between the hero and the list there are eight caption lines, all 11.5 px IBM Plex Mono in --text-muted, each a run of five to nine numbers separated by middle dots. Every number has the same weight, size, and colour as its label, so nothing is a landmark. The same counts appear three times: 18 summarized and 5 passed through are in the first caption, in the Code Mode line, and in the results filter row. Zero-valued items (0 policy pass-throughs, 0 patch cells, 0 polling cells) occupy the same space as the ones that carry information.

Fix: numbers become values in a labeled grid, label above in 10 px caps, value in 13.5 px mono primary. Each count appears once. Zeros stay visible but muted, matching the Attention lens rule that a missing indicator looks broken. The closed accordion row is the only place a section repeats its headline numbers.

F4The three savings terms are shown as three tiles, not as an equation

Savings is term 1 minus terms 2 and 3. The as-built record for the Pricing rail already documents that arithmetic, but the lens shows the terms as three equal tiles with no operators, and the total sits 60 px above them in a different type size. The reader has to do the subtraction to believe the headline.

Fix: lay the terms out as a subtraction that ends in the headline, with a proportional bar underneath that shows how much of the unfiltered cost was avoided. Five numbers become one sentence.

F5Context by turn is a 105 px band for a chart that usually has one column

The plot is 72 px tall, has its own header row, legend, caption, and two full-width rules. Columns are capped at 24 px, so a short thread draws a single orange bar in the far left of a 964 px empty box. The chart earns its place as a verification signal, but it is a sparkline, not a section.

Fix: 56 px plot in a 300 px column to the right of the headline. Columns stay 24 px max so a long thread still reads. Legend and the one-line caption stay; the section rules go.

Polish
F6The confidence chip is a box in a screen of no boxes

The green Directly observed chip is the only bordered, filled element in the hero and it is 38 px tall. It is important (it says whether the figure was measured or reconstructed) but a dot plus a line of text carries the same meaning at a third of the height, and it reads better placed under the bar as the basis of the figure.

What works

The orange headline figure is the right first read and the right colour. The observed-versus-unfiltered comparison line is the right second read. The filter row on the results, with counts in every pill and disabled pills for empty groups, is exactly right and is kept in every option. Money in mono, labels in Geist, holds throughout.

Proposals

Three layouts, same data, same tokens

All three keep the headline figure, the confidence signal, the Context by turn chart, and the filterable results list. They differ in where the reference numbers live. Each frame is 1000 × 700, the size that broke.

ALedger: equation hero, chart on the right, three folding rows, a gripRecommended

The hero becomes the same two-column grid the Incidents lens uses. The three terms read as a subtraction that ends in the headline, with a bar showing the avoided share. Decisions, Context boundaries, and Code Mode fold into single rows whose closed state carries the numbers you would scan for. Below the grip, the results get four full rows at 700 px and every pixel past that.
